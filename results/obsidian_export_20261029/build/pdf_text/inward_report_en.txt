## 第1页

Inward Breakthrough
Can Large Language Models Learn to Use
Existing Knowledge Through Their Own Signals?
Daniel Chen
Bocconi × HEC Paris
October 2026
Abstract
This study examines a knowledge-use gap: language models can answer two constituent factual questions sep-
arately yet fail to answer their composition. Measurements on Qwen3 support the existence of this gap. Training
on self-generated targets yields modest transfer gains, whereas shuffling targets severely damages performance.
Confidence filtering increases target accuracy but does not consistently outperform random selection. Gains from
correct and incorrect self-targets remain difficult to distinguish. These findings support further investigation of
internal knowledge use, but do not establish a general mechanism of autonomous inward improvement.
1 Research question and motivation
A model may identify a person’s country of birth and separately answer a question about that country’s at-
tributes, yet fail when asked the composed question directly. This distinguishes facts accessible through sep-
arate prompts from facts successfully retrieved and combined in the current task . We operationalize the
knowledge-use gap as the composition failure rate among items for which both individual hops are answered
correctly.[S0,S1]
“Outward improvement” adds external facts, gold answers, or task feedback. “Inward improvement” uses
the model’s own answers or internal signals to improve access to its existing knowledge. These approaches are
compatible. The narrow question here is whether self-generated targets, without directly using gold answers as
training targets, can improve compositional generalization. “Existing knowledge” is a behavioral definition, not
a complete account of internal factual storage.
Scope of the evidence
The report follows three stages: gap measurement and scoring checks; internal readout and inference-time
patching; and training controls involving self-targets, confidence selection, and shuffling. All verified numbers
are read from local files. Error bars represent sample standard deviations across training seeds. Unverified
results are recorded only in the handoff document, with their original result files unavailable. Source IDs map to
the accompanying source register; original files are not modified.

Full self-target training reaches 53.1 ± 0.9% on C, above the base model (Figure 2). Historical single-hop-only
training (49.8%) and gold-target training (56.8%) can be traced only to the handoff and remain unverified. Gold
training is a single run and cannot support a seed-stability comparison against three-seed means. [S0,S5,S6]
Retention is imperfect. The saved exp8 single-hop check covers only the fixed first 150 C items. Hop 1
changes from 84.7% to 86.4%, while hop 2 changes from 74.0% to 72.4% (post-training seed means). Hop 1
is broadly retained, but hop 2 declines slightly. It would be inaccurate to claim that all single-hop capabilities
remain unchanged.[S13]
Magnitude and significance. The C gain is a few percentage points, a modest effect. Item-level paired
improvements are not significant in every seed. Consistent directions do not substitute for broader stability
testing. Holding out both people and category families makes the gain more informative than training-item
memorization, but general task adaptation remains a plausible contributor. [S5,S6]
Takeaway. Self-target training supports limited compositional transfer; it does not establish sustained au-
tonomous improvement or the absence of forgetting.

Incorrect self-targets and shuffled incorrect targets use the same questions; shuffling changes question–target
correspondence within categories. Full shuffling reaches 5.2 ± 1.5% on C (exp9), and shuffling incorrect targets
likewise causes severe damage. This does not support the strong explanation that question-format familiarity
alone is sufficient. However, shuffling also introduces contradictory supervision and distributional disruption, so
the damage cannot be uniquely attributed to factual content. [S6,S7,S10,S11]
The incorrect-minus-correct self-target gain difference is -0.27 pp, with paired-seed 95% interval [-1.52, 0.98].
No stable difference is detected, but this is not an equivalence result . Neither improvement over the base sur-
vives the item-level Holm correction. Correct and incorrect groups contain different questions, leaving difficulty
and semantic-correctness confounds. [S7]
Takeaway. Question–self-target correspondence appears important, but the independent effects of factual cor-
rectness and consistency with the model’s own beliefs remain unresolved.

4 Discussion: supported findings, withdrawn claims, limits
Four findings currently supported:
1. Single-hop accessibility does not guarantee composition. Paired scaling and intermediate-fact context sug-
gest a knowledge-use bottleneck. [S1]
2. Bridge signals are often readable on failures; absent readable signals cannot explain all failures. This remains
correlational evidence.[S3,S9]
3. Self-target training yields modest average transfer on C; arbitrarily reassigning targets to questions severely
damages performance.[S5,S6,S7]
4. Target accuracy and downstream transfer are not simply monotonic: confidence-ranking ability does not
imply a stable selection advantage. [S5,S6]
Four strong claims withdrawn or no longer used:
1. “A hidden-layer readout shows that the model actually used that knowledge.” Causal identification is missing.
2. “More accurate training targets necessarily improve composition.” Filtering results do not support that ne-
cessity.
3. “Incorrect and correct targets work equally well, so consistency is the mechanism.” Nonsignificance estab-
lishes neither equivalence nor a unique mechanism.
4. “Inward training causes no forgetting and enables sustained self-improvement.” Single-hop decline is ob-
served; iteration and cross-family generality remain untested.
Limitations. Evidence covers one model family and one factual-composition benchmark. Few seeds, one fixed
split, and exploratory multiple comparisons limit inference. Gold-answer narrowness affects correctness labels;
the 120-item audit lacks independent human validation. First-token readouts do not establish causal use. Original
patching, single-hop-only, and gold-target files are unavailable, and those figures remain unverified. Different
training sizes and scoring versions limit cross-experiment comparisons. [S0,S1,S2,S5,S6,S7]
5 Next steps
1. Iterative self-training: regenerate targets using new people each round; compare with fresh base-model
training. Monitor confidence on errors, forgetting, and cumulative optimization budgets.
2. Cross-structure transfer: change chain length, relation types, and phrasing while matching data and opti-
mization steps.
3. Latent probes: combine readouts with causal interventions; control token overlap, decoder, and position
confounds.
4. A second model family: reproduce frozen-target, random-selection, shuffling, and correctness diagnostics.
5. Literature comparison: distinguish composition gaps [1], internal multi-hop timing [2,5], context distilla-
tion [3], and self-generated reasoning training [4].
